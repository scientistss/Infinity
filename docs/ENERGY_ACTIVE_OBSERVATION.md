# Active energy challenge: bounded native observation

This is a diagnostic of the already released
[`6af7a5e85a522ba89478c5e95d919a2c04fb2e55`](https://github.com/scientistss/Infinity/commit/6af7a5e85a522ba89478c5e95d919a2c04fb2e55),
version 0.6.32-alpha.1 / v9/r16. It changes no application source, save rule,
production rule or existing twelve-job acceptance workflow. Its standalone
workflow is not a new game release. A script or a successful domain preparation
does not by itself establish a native timing result.

## Inputs and comparison

Each profile independently begins with `createInitialState(20261013, 20261013)`
and follows actual paid APIs through a legal first curvature, challenge entry
and all four new home-planet completion proofs. No earned save or prior report
is supplied as input. The fixture is compressed domain-time preparation, not
wall-clock player interaction.

- Ordinary: one planet, all three mines at level one, a paid level-four solar
  plant, no stock grants, no outstanding construction or fleets.
- Capital-assisted: four planets and eight real empty transport fleets. Only
  receipt-backed wallet shortfalls may be supplied after the same natural
  checkpoint. Research, buildings, warehouses, units, colony departures and
  arrivals are actual paid engine operations. No levels, score, receipts,
  colonies, ships or flight times are inserted directly.

All planets have full energy efficiency in both variants. Warehouses have
headroom, production is positive and the active score is below its cap. The
control uses the actual quote, abandonment and SaveSession readback APIs;
its complete serialized state differs only by the removal of the active
challenge. This measures the whole application's active-state cost, not an
isolated accounting function.

There are three interleaved pairs per profile, in AB/BA/AB order: twelve fresh
contexts and thirty-six fixed windows. Each context observes overview,
curvature and fleet, with 600 ms warmup and a 2,000 ms measurement per page.
Six real navigation clicks and six real manual saves are retained per context.
Saves bracket each window but occur outside it and are not simultaneous with
the CDP counter readings. Save latency is end-to-end native action latency,
not isolated SaveSession execution time.

## Evidence and limits

Date, RAF, timers, storage and the game simulation remain native. No profiler
runs. Exact application HTTP/V8 bytes, all 62 production assets, release
metadata, game source, diagnostic source, browser/runtime identity and the
complete generated input transcript are retained. The browser's native
EventTiming threshold may omit fast inputs; omission is not reported as zero.
RAF callback observations are not proof of completed paint or frame rate.

An independent checker reopens raw counter pairs, native event records and
complete saves. It recomputes the fixed census and paired summaries. The exact
released reader checks every captured save outside the CPU windows. Independent
rational wallet differences give a conservative bound on per-contribution
score flooring; this is not a claim of exact mathematical equivalence between
arbitrary tick divisions or different JavaScript runtimes.

The native body has a 480-second total budget, a 90-second stalled-phase limit
and a 120-second individual-context horizon. Cleanup is separately bounded;
the CI job remains limited to fifteen minutes. Flights must remain in transit
throughout each measured context. Any arrival, warehouse saturation, capped
score, nonpositive production, changed census, catch-up-sized frame gap,
identity failure or incomplete sample fails qualification. Failed or slow
samples remain in the evidence; no automatic sample retry or selection is used.

Results apply only to these one-planet and four-planet/eight-fleet profiles.
They do not cover an active challenge at 100 planets/1,000 fleets, real mobile
devices, background operating-system suspension, long-term play or a general
performance improvement. No gameplay optimization is included in this stage.
